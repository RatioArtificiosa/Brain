# VNR Research Brief — Virtual Neural Runtime

**Project:** VNR (Virtual Neural Runtime) · Synthetic Cognitive Connectome Research Platform
**Author:** project lead + AI research staff · **Date:** 2026-09-14 · **Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** approved for execution
**Hardware target:** consumer desktop, 64 GB RAM, 8 GB VRAM (NVIDIA Quadro M5000, driver 537.99), Windows 11

## 1. Problem and opportunity

Whole-brain spiking simulation at human scale is out of reach for ordinary hardware: the reference digital-twin demonstration at 86 billion neurons required on the order of fourteen thousand GPUs. But three published results change the shape of the problem. First, Knight & Nowotny (2021, *Nature Computational Science*) showed procedural connectivity — generating synapses on demand instead of storing them — reaching 4.13 million neurons and 24.2 billion synapses on a single GPU. Second, the FlyWire consortium published the complete adult *Drosophila* connectome (~139,000 neurons, ~50M synapses, 8,400+ cell types). Third, Shiu et al. (2024, *Nature*) built a whole-brain leaky integrate-and-fire model from that connectivity plus neurotransmitter data that reproduces sensorimotor processing. The techniques exist. What does not exist is a platform that combines biologically-derived generative rules with aggressive active-state virtualization and measures exactly how far the compression can go before behavior changes. That platform is VNR.

## 2. Central hypothesis and experimental program

**Primary hypothesis:** a sparse, event-driven neural architecture with procedurally generated connectivity can preserve measurable network dynamics while keeping only a small active subset of the total virtual network resident in memory. **Secondary hypotheses:** (a) connectome-derived structure beats degree-matched random controls as a scaling substrate; (b) dynamic materialization/eviction cuts resident memory without proportional task loss; (c) learned high-value connections selectively promoted from procedural to explicit state outperform indiscriminate persistence under fixed budgets. The program is staged: reproduce the fly baseline as control (139K explicit neurons, ~800MB — trivially fits); validate determinism of materialization/eviction against the explicit reference; scale virtual size across 1×–100× with matched controls (random, degree-matched, motif-destroyed); then run cognitive tasks (associative learning, reversal, working memory, sequence prediction, ambiguity) identically at every scale. The single most important output is one graph: compression ratio versus behavioral fidelity, and the location of its knee. Secondary outputs: which scaling rules preserve motifs/modularity, and which eviction policies dominate. Strict rules apply throughout: hypothesis, observation, measured result, and interpretation are recorded separately; no claim of human equivalence is permitted; the language decoder sits outside the simulator as an observer, never inside the cognitive loop.

## 3. Architecture and feasibility on our hardware

VNR separates the *logical* brain from *resident* computation: virtual neuron IDs (deterministic uint64 from seed + lineage) and procedural connectivity rules live permanently; transient state (voltage, refractory, short-term currents) materializes only inside an active frontier and is garbage-collected with hysteresis on decay; learned state is triaged into structural (stable), long-term (compressed persistent), and transient (evictable) layers, with a synapse cache promoting repeatedly-learned procedural connections to explicit tracking. Memory budgets confirm feasibility: the fly baseline needs under 1GB; one million virtual neurons need ~64MB of state at full residency and single-digit MB at a realistic 1–5% active frontier — far inside 64GB RAM and ~5GB usable VRAM (after OS/CUDA/reserve). The binding constraint is event throughput, not memory (~10⁹ synaptic events/sec at scale), so Python orchestrates while compiled kernels compute — Phase 0 builds the NumPy reference oracle, and the PyTorch GPU backend is mandatory, not optional, from Phase 3. Known risks are priced in: plasticity-vs-determinism (replay tests use statistical tolerance once STDP is on), GeNN-on-Windows friction (GeNN deferred to WSL2; torch first), and FlyWire ingestion as a real work item (auth + chunked download). **Definition of done for V1:** load and analyze the real connectome, extract motifs, generate seeded synthetic expansions, simulate procedurally with materialization/eviction, demonstrate ≥10× synaptic compression at ≥95% behavioral fidelity on the four-way benchmark (explicit-dense vs sparse vs procedural vs procedural+virtualized), and emit reproducible reports. Positive or negative, the compression-vs-fidelity result is publishable; a null result that maps the failure boundary is explicitly acceptable science.
